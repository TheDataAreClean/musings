> _Today is the data India achieved independence from British rule. What stories have you grown up with around this day?_
> 
> – Prompt from [IndieWebClub Bangalore](https://blr.indiewebclub.org/)[ ](https://blr.indiewebclub.org/)meetup #32.

***

I have never truly felt connected to the idea and celebration of Independence Day in India. Probably because I have only interacted with stories about it. I am aware that it is a good thing to be independent of British rule, but the idea has always been an abstraction in my head. These days it has also become hard to feel independent in today's India, which has taken me even farther from the concept.

Today is also the day [The Partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) began. That is what I remember when I think of Independence Day. Growing up in my family, this was the part that felt more significant. My grandmother was 12 years old during the partition, and travelled through horrific circumstances, leaving behind a home that we now call Pakistan.

The 15th August of it all was always just a public holiday to me. Partition was the part that felt real — even without knowing the before, only the after, and how it shaped my family.

***

My grandmother used to tell us her partition stories when me and my siblings were kids. Much later in my life I realised how much trauma that must have left in a 12-year-old. She wanted to share it with all of us and make us a part of her life. 

Unfortunately I do not remember it all too well now, I was too young to soak it in properly. She tried to hand over her stories to us, and they slipped through anyway. We probably were not old enough to hold them the way they should've been held.

Growing older, I have come to better understood (or atleast imagine) how that time shaped her, and as a result my parents. The impact of it has reached me anyway, even if not in the way I would have preferred.

***

As she has grown older, I crave a lucid conversation with my grandmother now. I want to go back to our hometown and walk through it with her — the home she left, the journey she made to get here, the parts of it she could not fully share with us when we were younger. I want to sit with her, and archive our family's story before it's gone. Before she's gone.

I have been making some plans around this for nearly an year now. I want to recording her stories as audio, add pictures from family albums where I can find them. But I am not actually doing it, stopping myself from taking the leap.

---

Going back and spending time with my grandmother also means engaging with my family about it, which has never been an easy proposition for me.  It would means involving my family in the process, and I find it hard to trust that they will understand why I want to do this.

This hesitance and fear has existed for me nearly as long as the idea to archive my family's history. I keep putting it off, trading it for my comfort, over something I won't be able to get back later.

And that is exactly why I want to do this in the first place. Understanding partition and her life after it, through her eyes, might help me understand my own family a little better too. Where the distance came from. Why trust doesn't come easy. It is knowing like the story of the 12-year-old girl and the story of why we are the way we are the same story. 

I have just never gone looking for it. Maybe some part of me does not want to find out.
