It's 9:30 at night, and I open Instagram instead of reading the book in my hand. The first thing that pops up on my screen is a 30-second reel. It is a scene from the movie [_Hidden Figures_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Figures).

You probably know the scene. [Taraji P. Henson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraji_P._Henson), playing mathematician [Katherine Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Johnson), runs across the NASA campus in the rain to use the “coloured” bathroom. When her boss finally asks where she disappears to every day, she gives him a truth he was not ready to hear.

I think about the humiliation Johnson and so many others faced, the anger they must have felt, and the defiance it took to continue doing their work. I feel riled up every time I see it. It makes me appreciate the film, the performances and the lives of the people it is based on.

There is just one problem. I have never watched _Hidden Figures_.

Well, let me correct that. I have “seen” it. I have not watched it.

---

My Instagram algorithm has evolved into a different beast over the past few months. As I have tried to spend less time on the app, it has found newer, more devious ways to suck me back in.

And it is succeeding. I often find myself watching movies in 30-second instalments (sometimes television shows too). A dramatic confrontation. A clever chase scene. A powerful courtroom speech. A final act of revenge. Then another clip, and another, and another.

Sometimes I see so many clips from the same film that I can piece together the entire plot. I know the characters, the conflict and how it ends. I can appreciate the story and discuss its best scenes (not that I ever would).

This has become my primary relationship with Instagram.

Credit where it is due to whoever is clipping and packaging these stories, whether it is a person or an AI tool. The edits are remarkably effective. They give me just enough context to care, then end at exactly the point that makes it impossible not to watch the next clip.

Sometimes I open the comments and find bots talking to other bots, posting the same empty reactions. Somewhere among them, there will be a comment asking, “@grok, what movie is this?” Yep, even on Instagram.

That is usually the moment the spell breaks. That one comment gives me enough of an ick that I want to throw my phone away. At least until the next time I open the app.

---

Being addicted to short videos is a problem in itself, but what frustrates me more is the habit displaced by this mindless endeavour. 

I barely watch movies anymore and have seen only two in theatres over the past six years!

It sucks to admit that because I used to love going to the cinema. In college, I would watch as many as three movies a week, often by myself. I bunked numerous classes and failed a few exams, but I always made it to the theatre.

Now, even watching a movie at home feels difficult. The opening credits have barely begun before I feel the urge to reach for my phone and check another notification. Or even better, watch a 30-second clip of another movie while the one in front of me is about to start.

A two-hour story feels like a commitment, while Instagram guarantees shots of 30-second emotional highs on demand.

---

I don’t know exactly where I am going with this blog post. Maybe I needed to say this out loud and let go of some of the shame around it.

Maybe I also need help. Definitely need recommendations!

Recommendations on how to get past this addiction, and recommendations for good films. I like warm films, action films and animated films.

I promise to watch them for longer than 30 seconds. And when I finish, I’ll share my thoughts with you.
