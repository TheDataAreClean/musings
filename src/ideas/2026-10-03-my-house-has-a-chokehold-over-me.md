I had a plan for how I wanted to start October. My house had some inputs on said plan.

Things have been breaking around my house for the last couple of days. Extension cords shot, a water purifier out of action, and many electronics going through their moody teenage phase.

{% marginnote "heaviest rain of the year" %} i have not checked the data, so don't quote me on it. {% endmarginnote %}

A couple of days ago in Bangalore, we had what felt like the heaviest rain of the year. It started pouring in the evening, paused briefly, then returned for a thunderous night.

Anyway, September ended, and I am awake now – apparently for electrical repairs.

***

October felt important because life has been changing quite a bit for me lately. A while ago, I decided to step away from organising [VizChitra](https://vizchitra.com/) after helping run the conference for two years. Also after two years, my role at work is evolving into a more favourable shape.

These changes have opened up a lot of time in my life, and I have been asking myself what I want my days to look like going forward and, most importantly, how to make room to create for myself again.

As a way to explore this creativity again, I have been reading [_The Artist’s Way_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Artist%27s_Way) with a friend and doing the weekly exercises (keep an eye out for the weekly series on it). I felt committed to October as the beginning of this shift in my life, with a little more space for myself to be.

Instead, the start of the month has been choppy and tiring. My house had its own plans for the month, and it did not feel like informing me about them.

I keep wanting to treat all of this like a regular interruption, fix what needs fixing, then get back to my day. But I am increasingly unsure that if one ever truly “gets back to their day.” while also inhabiting a house.

---

{% marginnote "half-parked" %} the constant anticipation. {% endmarginnote %}

There is always another fix waiting in the wings: a fan that starts clicking in the middle of a call, the cabinet hinge that suddenly wants to squeak. My attention has been permanently half-parked on just looking after the house for years.

I am not sure there was ever a stretch when the house didn't need something. I cannot even remember many conversations from my childhood with my parents, where repairs were not a part of it.

And then there are the jobs I have invented for myself. My magnum opus is reorganising the bookshelf every six months. Either because the books on my shelves have changed, or the way I organise them in my head has shifted, so the bookshelf feels like it needs to reflect that.

Nobody is making me do that one. I just want the shelves to make sense again!

---

I could blame my current predicament on the limitations of this particular house, or maybe if I had more money for someone to take care of everything full-time I would not feel this. On a tiring day like the one I just had, both explanations are tempting.

But I love this house. I do not particularly want a bigger one. And I especially do not want to hand over all the looking after it, either. I like doing things myself, knowing where things are, deciding how they should work, making this place feel like mine.

I just wish it did not get so overwhelming sometimes.

---

Some of this feeling of being overwhelmed probably comes from how I plan my days. I fill them to the brim, assigning every available bit of time to something I want to do. There’s very little room for ambiguity. Certainly no slot labelled “water purifier not purifying”.

And when something breaks, I stop and deal with it – because that’s what living in a house requires, and because I care about this one.

I am tired at the end of all the fixes, and annoyed that I have not done what I planned, even though I have spent the day doing something that matters to me.

---

I do not have a neat conclusion, and not looking for solutions, either. Maybe I just want things to stop breaking for a while and catch a breath.

One thing I would appreciate is a significant amount of uninterrupted time reading, writing, or making things.

But I think I need to leave a little more room in my plans for the home I actually live in. Including the inconvenient parts of looking after it. I love this house. Sometimes I would like for it to love me back by keeping all its appliances working. For now, I will settle for a functioning water purifier.
