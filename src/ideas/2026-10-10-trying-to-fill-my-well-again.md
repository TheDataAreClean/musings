Last week I spectacularly stammered my way through a conversation at work. The frustrating part was, this was something I had researched and worked on for weeks. Something I should have been able to explain in my sleep. But the moment I joined the Slack huddle, the words scattered, tumbling out of my mouth and onto the floor.

I have been feeling a serious loss of confidence over the last few months. I have spent a large part of my time over the last two years building [successful](https://www.dataforindia.com/) [things](https://vizchitra.com/) and watching them progress in the world and propagate a belief system I hold. But for all the plaudits the work received, it never truly felt mine.

The work used to go out, and people were excited, even ecstatic, about it; but my well still sat empty.

I am proud of these things! But they did not give me the same feeling I get when doing something just for myself. Like a piece of writing, which I have been doing [more](https://musings.thedataareclean.com/) of recently. Or a [project](https://www.instagram.com/36truths/) I poured my heart into a while ago. I keep finding joy in the things I did for myself, long after they have been completed.

***

A few weeks ago, in a long conversation with a very dear friend and fellow artist, we decided to read [_The Artist's Way_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Artist%27s_Way) by Julia Cameron together. Another friend recommended this to me nearly six years ago, and it has been on my radar since, but I just never picked it up.

The current crisis of confidence pushed me into reading it. Over the last week or so, I have been working through the chapters, getting a feel for the book. Like every other book in this genre, it starts with a few detailed, slightly long, and somewhat praise-y pages on why “this” is the best method yet. It also has a few too many references to God for my belief system's liking.

But as I read through and learn more about the intention behind the book, it has aligned well with me. I consider myself an artist, and everyone around me one too. Maybe repeating it for twelve weeks will finally make me believe it.

The opening chapters ask for something specific. Show up for the next twelve weeks and do the two basic tools the book is built on: three morning pages every day and one artist date every week, trusting that the wanting to create counts on its own.

A good chunk of the early chapters is about the inner critic, the voice that has been running commentary in my head for months now, and the book asks you to catch it and shut it down before it gets a vote. After months of my well feeling empty, a book with an entire idea built around filling the well back up felt aimed directly at me.

***

This morning I did my first-ever morning pages. Three pages longhand before anything else, no editing, no rereading (I really wanted to). The thing I am most proud of is that I only got distracted twice during those 28 minutes. I am hopeful and skeptical in equal measure, trying to respect the wisdom of the book and keep going. My one worry is how this sits alongside all the other writing in my life.

Honestly, I am out of other ideas too. I want to feel good about the work I create and bring into this world, and this book is the best plan I have right now.

The artist dates are something I am genuinely looking forward to. Something I do for myself anyway, but now it has a name. Hoping it gives my [photography](https://photos.thedataareclean.com/) more dedicated time, instead of the spare scraps of attention it has been surviving on.

***

As I wrap up this blog, I feel an urge to keep creating and to pay close attention to the art around me. I am reminded that art is especially necessary now, when those in power are willing to shut down the country's capital and fundamental institutions are crumbling. Art can help hold those in power to account. So the morning pages continue tomorrow.
