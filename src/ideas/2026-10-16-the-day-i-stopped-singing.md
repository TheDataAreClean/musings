I have a fairly deep voice. A voice that travels and fills a room. It is truly a joy using my vocal cords and feel them reverberate every time I speak.

Someone once told me that people pay attention when I speak, and that I should use my voice to that purpose more often. I never felt comfortable with that idea, and chose not to pursue it.

There is nothing wrong with people listening to me; I just had a different dream for my voice. I used to love singing. I still love singing, it is just slightly different now.

***

At school, I was part of the Indian Music group, and music period was easily my favourite part of the week. Singing my heart out, I loved hearing my voice bounce around in that bright corner room of the school building.

{% marginnote "Singing" %} i also tried learning the bongo, though that never progressed much beyond hyperactive teenage boy being given permission to bang on drums. {% endmarginnote %}

My Indian music teacher encouraged and supported my singing like no one else and deserves a special mention here. Unfortunately, I am forgetting her name right now, which is a terrible way to give someone a special mention.

But the seeds of my singing were truly planted at home. My dad used to play [_Kishore Kumar_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishore_Kumar) and [_Mohammed Rafi_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Rafi) cassettes every morning, and I would sing along. As far as I was concerned, these were the best songs there had ever been, and loved singing them. In the infinite wisdom of youth, I imagined I would only get better at singing those songs.

{% marginnote "best" %} this still holds true today. {% endmarginnote %}

***

And then the worst thing that could happen to my singing aspirations, happened. I hit puberty. My voice grew deeper and coarser and could no longer carry the songs that had brought me so much joy. Cracks had appeared in my voice, and the notes were leaking out.

For the first few weeks, I was completely thrown off. Losing a big part of my identity, teenage me had no idea what to do about it. I tried changing the songs. [_Kailash Kher_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailash_Kher) became a favourite. His songs gave my increasingly coarse voice somewhere to go. Then it was [_Sukhwinder_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhwinder_Singh), [_Rabbi Shergill_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi_Shergill), and an optimistic attempt at [_Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusrat_Fateh_Ali_Khan).

{% marginnote "Kailash Kher" %} fun fact: he used to live two streets down from my house and taught music at a neighbourhood school. {% endmarginnote %}

But I was grasping at straws. My voice was changing faster than I could learn and adapt to new genres of music.

***

The turning point came in ninth standard, during auditions for an inter-house music competition. The teacher doing the selections was not my regular music teacher. She seemed surprised that I sang at all and dismissed my audition with little encouragement.

That memory still stings. I shed a few tears that day, much to the surprise of my friends. They did not seem to think it was a particularly big deal. It was for a kid whose entire life was filled with singing.

My Indian music teacher, realising I had not been selected, assumed there had been a mistake and asked me to join. I was grateful to join, but my voice was sticking out like a sore thumb. Two days before the competition, the other teacher who had rejected me earlier stopped by during a practice session and thought the sound was a little off.

To find out why, she pointed at me and asked me to sing alone. As my cracked voice came through, the whole group looked at me with horror. She asked me to stop, then had the rest of the group sing without me. After hearing the group sing without me, she asked me to leave.

My house went on to win the competition. If I ever needed a signal that the sun had set on my singing ambitions, this felt fairly unambiguous. The timing could have been kinder, though.

***

My love for singing endures even today. I sing by myself, and sometimes around people I am close to. But I have not let my voice loose the same way I used to in nearly two decades.

Somewhere along the way, I became careful with something I had never had to think about before. While I like my voice now, I am curious about what it would feel like to give it a little room again.

I think of _Kailash Kher_ again, and of “[_Allah Ke Bande_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KSwd2fYX9vg&list=RDKSwd2fYX9vg)” the first song I turned to when my voice began to crack.

> _“Toota toota ek parinda aise toota, ki phir ud na paaya…”_
> A bird was shattered, shattered so badly that it could never fly again…
>
> _“Loota loota kisne usko aise loota, ki phir ud na paaya…”_
> Who robbed it, who plundered it so completely that it could never fly again…
>
> _“Girta hua hoon aasmaan se, aakar gira zameen par…”_
> Falling from the sky, it came crashing down to earth…
>
> _“Khaabon mein phir bhi baadal hi the, woh kehta raha magar…”_
> Yet clouds still filled its dreams, even as it kept saying…
>
> _“Ke Allah ke bande hans de, Allah ke bande, Allah ke bande hans de…”_
> O servant of God, smile… O servant of God, just smile…
>
> _“Jo bhi ho, kal phir aayega…”_
> Whatever happens, tomorrow will come again…

I will go try that now.
