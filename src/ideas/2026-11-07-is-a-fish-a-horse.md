## Anatomically

- **No.** A [horse is a hoofed mammal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse). A fish generally has fins. You will know when you see it.
- **Yes.** Both have [backbones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate). Something to bring up at the holiday family dinner.
- **Maybe.** A [seahorse has a horse-shaped head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse#Description). The rest of it appears to have been assembled from a separate mood board.

## Respiratorily

- **No.** Horses have [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_system_of_the_horse). Most fish use [gills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_gill). Please do not test either at home.
- **Yes.** Both need oxygen. A surprisingly demanding shared hobby.
- **Maybe.** [Some fish have lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lungfish#Lungs). They continue to be fish. An appeal is underway.

## Evolutionarily

- **No.** Today’s [horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_the_horse) did not evolve from today’s seahorses. The family dinner would be even more awkward.
- **Yes.** Go back far enough and horses and fish share [fish-like ancestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish#Phylogeny). The family tree has roots underwater.
- **Maybe.** In a strict evolutionary classification, [tetrapods descend from fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish#Phylogeny). So a horse could be called a very specialised fish, if you are ready to have that argument.

## Geographically

- **No.** [Horses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse) generally live on land; [seahorses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse#Habitat) live in water. The property prices are usually very different.
- **Yes.** Both can be found around [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse#Digestion). One grazes on it; the other may [cling to it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse#Habitat).
- **Maybe.** [Mudskippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudskipper) are fish that spend time on land. They appear to be doing well to afford these property rates.

## Socially

- **No.** In seahorses, [the male carries the babies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse#Reproduction). For horses, that would be a surprising announcement.
- **Yes.** Horses live in [social groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_behavior#As_herd_animals); some fish swim in [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoaling_and_schooling). Both know a thing or two about groupthink.
- **Maybe.** Seahorses [dance during courtship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse#Courtship). Perhaps they are looking for a stable relationship.

## Culinarily

- **No.** [Horses eat plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse#Digestion). [Seahorses eat tiny crustaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse#Feeding_habits). The holiday dinner menu may require some negotiation.
- **Yes.** People eat [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_uses_of_fish#For_food) and, in some places, [horse meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_meat). Neither is stoked about this arrangement.
- **Maybe.** [Grass carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_carp#Ecology) are fish that eat aquatic plants. The horse might finally find someone willing to order the salad with them.

## Linguistically

- **No.** In Hindi, a hippopotamus is called [दरियाई घोड़ा](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%88_%E0%A4%98%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%A1%E0%A4%BC%E0%A4%BE), literally “river horse”. It is neither a horse nor a fish. Apparently, putting a horse in the name does not settle much.
- **Yes.** [Seahorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse). It is right there in the name. Case closed, provided we never examine the rest of the word.
- **Maybe.** _Hippocampus_, the seahorse’s genus, comes from Greek words for [horse and sea monster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse#Etymology). The branding team worked overtime to get this one right.

## Mythologically

- **No.** The actual [seahorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seahorse) is small, fishy and not known to pull divine chariots. Its cultural potential remains unverified.
- **Yes.** Poseidon’s chariot was sometimes drawn by [hippocampi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocampus_\(mythology\)#Greek_and_Roman), creatures with the front of a horse and the tail of a fish. Greek mythology took one look at the IWCB prompt and said, “Sure, why not?”
- **Maybe.** In Hindu mythology, Vishnu appears as [Matsya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsya), a fish, and [Hayagriva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayagriva), a horse-headed form. Vishnu seems to have kept both options open.

## Astrologically

- **No.** [Pisces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisces_\(astrology\)) is represented by two fish, not horses. Even astrology can tell the difference.
- **Yes.** Fish have [Pisces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisces_\(astrology\)) in Western astrology; horses have a sign in the [Chinese zodiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_\(zodiac\)). Their horoscopes will have to compare calendars first.
- **Maybe.** The Chinese zodiac includes a [Water Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_\(zodiac\)#Years_and_the_Five_Elements). The stars have not quite said yes, but they have put the horse in water.
